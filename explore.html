CrossSense

When the signal gets noisy.

CrossSense is my doctoral research at UMKC. I studied Bluetooth proximity sensing and agricultural vision, asking how much we can rely on a model when its input or surroundings change.

Doctoral researchQuality-aware AICross-domain sensing
Explore signal qualitySynthetic illustration

Move the slider to explore the input before a prediction is made.

This drawing uses arbitrary noise units. It is not a CrossSense model, measured result, or safety recommendation.

How I approached it

The framework checks the input and its context before interpreting a prediction. Its six elements cover signal quality through to the response. The same structure can be used in both studies, but the models and calibration still have to suit each problem.

Explore the six elements
  1. Quality estimation. Assess the input.
  2. Context inference. Understand the setting.
  3. Signal stabilization. Address fluctuations where appropriate.
  4. Task inference. Make the domain-specific prediction.
  5. Uncertainty calibration. Interpret confidence using evidence.
  6. Risk-limiting policy. Connect uncertainty to the response.
The two studies

In the Bluetooth study, the input is radio signal strength. In the vision study, it is an image. Both raise the question of when a prediction is reliable enough to use.

Related work includes AlertBLE, AIBLE, and cross-architecture agricultural IoT vision research.

Universal Metallurgical Process Simulator

Comparing process designs.

I conceived and built the Universal Metallurgical Process Simulator while working with Critical Materials Crossroads. It lets process-design choices be examined alongside their financial implications.

Industrial optimizationConcept & deliveryDecision support
Choose a perspectiveConceptual · not simulator output

How much can be recovered?

Recovery is one quantity the simulator examines. A process with higher recovery still needs to be considered alongside its cost and environmental impact.

Inside the simulator

The calculations start with physics-based mass and energy balances. Genetic algorithms and NSGA-II explore competing choices, with feasibility checks. I included capital and operating cost analysis, as well as net present value and internal rate of return.

Methods and implementation

I added sensitivity and Monte Carlo analysis to examine changes in the assumptions. The application also includes LLM explanations and progress updates through Flask and REST APIs.

About the example

The curve is a sketch to introduce the problem, not output from the simulator. This page does not claim a measured recovery improvement or cost saving.
